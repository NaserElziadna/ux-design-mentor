# Evil by Design - Chris Nodder

Psychology-based persuasion techniques for user interaction design; when and how to ethically influence behavior.

## Core Concept: Persuasion in Design

Users' decisions are influenced by psychology, not just logic.

Ethical design acknowledges this and guides users toward beneficial choices.

**Evil by Design = Using psychology ethically**
(Not deception, but understanding how people decide)

## Persuasion Principles

### 1. Reciprocity
People feel obligated to return favors or give back.

**Ethical application:**
- Provide free value first
- Help users before asking for help
- Give genuine benefits
- Create goodwill through generosity

**Unethical application:**
- Trick users into obligation
- Bait and switch
- Hidden costs

### 2. Commitment & Consistency
Once committed, people want to be consistent with that commitment.

**Ethical application:**
- Start with small commitments
- Make commitments public
- Align product with values
- Show consistency in behavior

**Unethical application:**
- Trap users in agreements
- Use peer pressure manipulatively
- Subtle scope creep

### 3. Social Proof
People look to others' behavior to guide their own.

**Ethical application:**
- Show real social proof (authentic testimonials)
- Highlight what responsible people do
- Make desired behavior visible
- Use peer influences positively

**Unethical application:**
- Fake testimonials
- Fake social proof
- Pressure through false consensus

### 4. Authority
People trust and follow perceived experts.

**Ethical application:**
- Feature real expert credentials
- Be transparent about expertise
- Provide evidence and sources
- Build earned authority

**Unethical application:**
- False credentials
- Appeal to fake authority
- Misleading qualifications

### 5. Liking
People prefer those who are similar, attractive, or complimentary.

**Ethical application:**
- Personalize experiences appropriately
- Be authentic and genuine
- Share common values
- Provide genuine compliments

**Unethical application:**
- Excessive flattery
- False personalization
- Manipulation through likes

### 6. Scarcity
Limited availability creates urgency.

**Ethical application:**
- Real scarcity (actual limited supply)
- Honest time constraints
- Genuine seasonal offers
- Transparent inventory

**Unethical application:**
- Artificial scarcity
- Fake countdowns
- False "last one" claims

## Default Options

What you don't choose matters as much as what you do.

**The power of defaults:**
- Most users don't change defaults
- Default = tacit endorsement
- Affects majority of users

**Ethical defaults:**
- Opt-in for data sharing (not opt-out)
- Default to privacy
- Default to safest option
- Be transparent about why

**Unethical defaults:**
- Auto-check boxes
- Opt-out instead of opt-in
- Hidden costs as default
- Confusing default selections

## Progress & Motivation

Show progress to maintain motivation.

**Motivation principles:**
- Make progress visible
- Show how close to goal
- Celebrate milestones
- Reduce friction to completion

**Application:**
- Progress bars
- Step completion
- Milestone celebrations
- Streak counters (with break insurance)

## Emotional Engagement

Emotions drive behavior more than logic.

**Emotional design:**
- Create positive emotions
- Reduce negative emotions
- Build emotional connection
- Make experiences memorable

**Application:**
- Delight in small interactions
- Humor where appropriate
- Celebrate achievements
- Acknowledge frustrations

## Ethical Persuasion Framework

**Before using persuasion, ask:**

1. **Is this in the user's best interest?**
   - Would I want this done to me?
   - Does it help the user achieve their goals?
   - Does it align with their values?

2. **Am I being transparent?**
   - Would users understand if they knew the psychology?
   - Is scarcity real?
   - Are claims backed by evidence?
   - Are limitations disclosed?

3. **Is there informed consent?**
   - Do users understand what they're agreeing to?
   - Can they easily change their mind?
   - Are terms clear and accessible?

4. **Would I defend this publicly?**
   - Would I explain this to regulators?
   - Would press coverage be embarrassing?
   - Would users feel respected or manipulated?

## Common Unethical Patterns

### Dark Pattern 1: Hidden Costs
Real charges revealed only near end of transaction.

**Fix:** Disclose all costs upfront, include in total

### Dark Pattern 2: Confirmshaming
Buttons with emotional guilt ("Yes, I'm okay with ads" vs "I hate free content")

**Fix:** Neutral options for both choices

### Dark Pattern 3: Forced Continuity
Free trial becomes paid without warning.

**Fix:** Clear countdown, require explicit permission, easy cancellation

### Dark Pattern 4: Roach Motel
Easy to join, difficult to leave.

**Fix:** Exit should be as easy as entry

### Dark Pattern 5: Trick Questions
Confusing wording makes users agree to things they don't intend.

**Fix:** Clear, straightforward language

- [ ] Is data handling ethical?

## Key Takeaway

> "Just because you can persuade users doesn't mean you should." - Chris Nodder

Ethical persuasion design:
- Respects user autonomy
- Is transparent about techniques
- Serves user goals first
- Builds long-term trust

The "evil" in Evil by Design is acknowledging the power of psychology and using it responsibly.
